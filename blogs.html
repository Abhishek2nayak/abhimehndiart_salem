Mehndi tips & inspiration

Notes from the studio on aftercare, styles and getting the most out of your appointment.

AFTERCARE · 5 MIN READ
Getting the Darkest Henna Stain

The single biggest factor in how dark your mehndi turns out isn't the henna cone — it's what you do in the hours after application. Here's what actually makes a difference.

Before your appointment

Skip lotions, oils and exfoliation on the day of your mehndi. Clean, dry, product-free skin lets the henna paste sit closer to the surface and stain more evenly. If you're getting bridal mehndi, avoid waxing or threading the exact area the day before, as freshly irritated skin can affect how the paste bonds.

While the paste dries

Let the paste dry naturally for at least 2–4 hours — longer for bridal designs. We'll usually dab a lemon-sugar mix over the dried paste partway through; this keeps it from cracking and flaking off too early, which is one of the most common reasons a stain comes out patchy.

After you scrape it off

Don't wash the area with water for at least 12 hours. Scrape off the dried paste (never rinse it away) and dab on a little clove oil, eucalyptus oil or a warm mustard-oil-and-sugar mix — the natural stain continues to darken for 24–48 hours after the paste comes off, and heat helps that process along. Avoid soap, chlorine and hand sanitiser during this window if you can.

Follow these steps and most clients see their design deepen from a bright orange to a rich maroon-brown over two days — exactly the shade brides are hoping for on their wedding morning.

BRIDAL · 6 MIN READ
Bridal Mehndi Trends for This Wedding Season

Every wedding season brings a slightly different mood to bridal consultations. Here's what Salem brides have been asking for lately.

Personalised portrait panels

A small, hidden panel on the palm depicting the couple, the wedding date, or a favourite shared memory has become one of our most requested bridal add-ons. It's usually tucked into the centre of the palm so it stays private until the groom goes looking for it.

Negative space, used deliberately

Rather than filling every inch of the hand, many brides now prefer dense floral clusters with breathing room between them. It photographs beautifully against jewellery and keeps the overall look elegant rather than heavy.

Matching bridal party sets

Coordinated, lighter versions of the bridal pattern for sisters and close friends is a lovely (and increasingly common) request — it ties the whole wedding party's photos together without asking anyone else to sit for four hours.

Fusion borders

A traditional Tamil floral centre with a fine Arabic-style trailing border along the fingers is the most popular fusion combination we're drawing this year — it keeps the ceremony feeling rooted while giving the design a modern edge.

If you have a Pinterest board or a few reference photos, bring them to your consultation — we'll build a design plan around the ones you love most.

STYLES · 4 MIN READ
Arabic vs. Traditional: Which Suits Your Event?

Clients often ask us to simply "pick whichever looks nicer," but the right style really depends on your event, your outfit and how much time you have to sit.

Choose Arabic mehndi when…

You want a bold, striking look without a long sitting. Arabic designs use large floral motifs and trailing vines with open space between them, so they apply quickly (often 45 minutes to an hour per hand) and read beautifully in photos from a distance — ideal for a sangeet, a friend's wedding, or when you're one of many guests waiting for a turn.

Choose traditional mehndi when…

The occasion calls for density and detail — pujas, sadangu, a traditional wedding ceremony, or any function where the mehndi itself is part of the ritual. Fine paisleys, peacocks and temple borders fill the hand completely and take longer to apply, but the intricacy is exactly what makes them feel special for milestone events.

Or mix the two

A traditional centrepiece motif with Arabic-style trailing fingers gives you the best of both — visual impact plus a manageable sitting time. This is one of our most requested combinations for engagement ceremonies.

PREPARATION · 3 MIN READ
How to Prepare Your Skin Before a Mehndi Appointment

A little preparation goes a long way toward a darker, longer-lasting stain and a smoother appointment overall.

1–2 days before

Do any waxing, threading or hair removal at least a day in advance so your skin has time to calm down. Gently exfoliate your hands and feet the night before — removing dead skin helps the henna bond to fresher skin underneath.

On the day

Avoid moisturiser, oil or sunscreen on the areas being designed — a barrier between your skin and the paste is the most common reason for a patchy stain. Wear something you can slip on without using your hands afterwards, and plan to keep your hands free for at least 3–4 hours post-application.

What to bring

Reference photos, a rough idea of coverage (just fingertips, full palm, or up to the elbow), and any specific motifs or initials you'd like hidden in the design. The more direction you can give us upfront, the less back-and-forth we need mid-appointment.

OCCASIONS · 3 MIN READ
Mehndi Etiquette for Baby Showers & Valaikaappu

Valaikaappu and seemantham mehndi has its own rhythm — the focus is on comfort for the mother-to-be, not marathon sittings.

Keep it short and gentle

We usually recommend one to two hours of coverage on the hands and forearms, with breaks built in. Long sittings in one position aren't ideal in later pregnancy, so we design around comfortable, supported seating.

Motifs that suit the occasion

Bangles, lotus buds, peacocks and soft floral vines are traditional favourites — symbols of fertility and new beginnings rather than the dense bridal patterns used for weddings.

Booking for guests too

Many families ask us to set up a small mehndi corner for guests at the same event. We're happy to bring an assistant artist for larger gatherings — just let us know the expected guest count when you enquire.
